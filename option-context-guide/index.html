Option Context Guide

Options, quantity, and price only mean something together. Re-read all three against the option you have selected right now.

Why a number alone is not enough

A price written in the spreadsheet exists inside an option. The same item can carry different prices for different colors, sizes, versions, or quantities. A number read without its option context can point at the wrong variant.

1. Color

Different colorways are often separate variants with separate stock and sometimes separate pricing. Confirm the color you selected is the color the clue describes.

2. Size

Size changes which unit you are buying. A price quoted for one size may not hold for another, and availability can differ by size.

3. Version

Listings often split into versions — an older batch, a revised model, a different material. The version must match the clue you intend to act on.

4. Quantity

Quantity changes what a price means. A per-unit price and a bulk price are different statements, and shipping can shift with quantity.

5. Price

Only after color, size, version, and quantity are fixed does the price become meaningful. Re-read the total against the exact selection before relying on it.

Margin note Fix the options first, then read the price. Reading the price before the options is how a clue gets mistaken for a confirmed listing.
?Open question: does the price on the Sheet apply to the option you actually selected?

A worked reading

Consider a jacket listed with a price that seems lower than expected.

  1. Color: the low price belongs to a discontinued colorway.
  2. Size: the clue’s size is out of stock; only other sizes remain.
  3. Version: the price is for an older version, not the current one.
  4. Quantity: the price assumes a multi-unit order, not a single item.

Each of these changes what the number means. Re-reading the option context first would catch the mismatch before any decision.

Evidence-reading example — not live listing data, verification, or a saved record.
