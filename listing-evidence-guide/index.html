Listing Evidence Guide

Separate a source clue from the current page and the items still waiting to be confirmed. A spreadsheet row is where reading begins, not where it ends.

Three layers of a listing clue

Every row in the JoyaGoo spreadsheet points somewhere. To read it well, keep three layers apart: the source clue, the current page, and the to-be-confirmed details.

1. The source clue

The source clue is the row itself — a reference compiled in a shared document, or a link copied from a category page. It tells you where to look, not what you will find. A clue has an origin, and knowing that origin shapes how much weight to give it.

  • A clue that comes straight from the Sheet is a pointer collected by someone else.
  • A clue you reached through a category page carries the context of that grouping.
  • A clue taken directly from a target page is the closest to the item itself.
Margin note The closer a clue sits to the target page, the less it depends on someone else’s snapshot. Prefer the closest clue you can find.

2. The current page

Once you open a link, the page you actually land on becomes the second layer. Check its title, its domain, and where the link finally resolved. Redirects and changed listings can move you without warning.

  • Read the page title — does it match the item you meant to inspect?
  • Check the domain — is this the destination you expected?
  • Note the final URL — where did the link actually settle?

3. The to-be-confirmed

The third layer is everything you still need to verify: the exact option, the price in context, the image stage, and any open questions. These stay open until the target page resolves them.

?Open question: does the page you opened match the item the clue described?

A worked reading, step by step

Imagine a Sheet row that lists a bag. Walking it through the three layers looks like this:

  1. Source: the row was compiled in the shared Sheet and grouped under a category. It is a pointer.
  2. Page: opening the link lands on a store page whose title names the bag. The domain matches the agent route you expected.
  3. Confirm: the color, size, and price shown now must be re-read against your selected option — they are not yet settled.

Evidence-reading example — not live listing data, verification, or a saved record.

Where a clue stops being enough

A clue is enough to start, not to decide. Stop and re-verify when the page identity does not match, when an option conflicts, or when an unknown stays unresolved. Each of those is a signal to go back one layer rather than move forward.
